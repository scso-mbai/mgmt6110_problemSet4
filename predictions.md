## 1. My product
- Live address: https://mgmt6110problemset4.vercel.app/
- Who it is for, and the one job it does for them: For trading card players in Singapore, it shows which nearby hobby store has a single card in stock, at what price and how far away, so they can reserve it and collect it.
- Health check from Step 1, on Saturday 26 September 2026, about 1:50 AM (Singapore time): `GET /api/health` returned **503** with `{"keyConfigured":false,"upstreamAnswered":false,"upstreamStatus":null,"error":"The 'location' environment variable is missing or empty."}`. The first request after several idle hours took about 790 ms; the next three took about 290 ms each. `GET /api/location` with no coordinates also returned 503 with the same message.
- Devices and browsers I used for this evaluation: laptop Chromium (the Claude desktop app's built-in browser) at a 708 px wide window and at a 375 × 812 phone viewport, with location permission blocked; the 3G reload was run in headless Chromium using Chrome's 3G throttling values (2,000 ms latency, 400 kbit/s).

## 2. My findings

### Finding 1
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — card detail screen (Celestial Archive of Spells), phone viewport
- What I did, what I saw: I opened a card from the catalogue, added 5 copies to the cart, then pressed the browser's Back button. The browser left the site entirely and went to the page I was on before. Pressing Forward reloaded the catalogue with the cart back at 0.
- Which heuristic: 3, User control and freedom
- Screen or system: Screen. Every screen shares one address, so Back has nothing inside the app to return to. The front end could push a history entry for each screen with what it already has.
- Severity, and why: 3. Frequency drove it: Back (or the swipe-back gesture) is the most common thing a phone user does mid-task, and the whole cart is lost with no warning.
- The repair: Back on the card detail or cart screen returns to the previous screen inside the app, and the cart is still there.

### Finding 2
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — any screen, with items in the cart
- What I did, what I saw: I added a card to the cart and reloaded the page. The page came back on the catalogue and the cart badge showed 0.
- Which heuristic: 5, Error prevention
- Screen or system: Screen. The cart lives only in memory; the screen could keep it in the browser's own storage without any server.
- Severity, and why: 3. Impact drove it: an accidental reload, a phone killing the tab in the background, or a slow connection retry wipes everything the visitor chose.
- The repair: After a reload the cart holds the same cards, quantities and stores as before.

### Finding 3
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — top bar on every screen, phone viewport (375 px) and a 708 px laptop window, with location blocked
- What I did, what I saw: With location blocked, the "Your Base" pill fills with the long message "Location access is unavailable or has been blocked." The page became 438 px wide on a 375 px screen, and the Cart button sat completely off the right edge (at a 708 px window it also sat past the edge). The only way I reached the cart was the "View Cart" link in the toast after adding a card.
- Which heuristic: 1, Visibility of system status
- Screen or system: Screen. The top bar could shorten the message or let it wrap, and keep the Cart button always in view.
- Severity, and why: 3. Frequency drove it: many visitors block location, and on a phone the cart count and the way to check out disappear for all of them.
- The repair: At 375 px, with any location message, the Cart button and its count are fully visible and the page does not scroll sideways.

### Finding 4
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — card detail screen, store list, "Hold" button
- What I did, what I saw: I pressed "Hold" on Merlion Card Sanctum. A message said "Reserved 1x … at Merlion Card Sanctum … (5 remaining in stock)." Nothing else changed: the stock still said 5, nothing went into the cart, and the hold appears nowhere afterwards.
- Which heuristic: 1, Visibility of system status
- Screen or system: System. A real hold needs a back end that tells the store and reduces stock; the screen alone cannot make one. The screen could, however, stop claiming a reservation it has not made.
- Severity, and why: 3. Impact drove it: a visitor who believes the card is held may travel to the store and find it sold.
- The repair: Either the hold is real and shows up somewhere the visitor can find it again, or the button and message no longer say "reserved".

### Finding 5
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — cart screen, "Check Out" button
- What I did, what I saw: I pressed "Check Out ($46.00)" once. It went straight to "Order Confirmed! Your single card reservations have been dispatched to the 1 store." There was no review step, no name or contact detail, and no request left the browser.
- Which heuristic: 5, Error prevention
- Screen or system: Both. The screen could add a confirmation step on its own; actually dispatching an order to a store needs a back end.
- Severity, and why: 3. Impact drove it: one mistaken tap produces a confirmation for an order that no store will ever receive.
- The repair: Checkout asks the visitor to confirm the stores, the total and how to contact them, and only says "confirmed" when a store has really been told.

### Finding 6
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — card detail screen, "Add to Cart" button and its message
- What I did, what I saw: I pressed "Add to Cart" twice quickly: the cart went from 0 to 2, but the message said "Added 1x". I kept pressing until the cart reached the store's stock of 5; further presses changed nothing, yet each still showed "Added 1x … to your shopping cart!"
- Which heuristic: 1, Visibility of system status
- Screen or system: Screen. It already knows the cart quantity and the stock limit.
- Severity, and why: 2. Frequency drove it: double taps are common on phones, but the visitor can still fix the quantity in the cart.
- The repair: The message states the quantity now in the cart, and says so plainly when the store has no more copies.

### Finding 7
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — top bar and the "Your Base Status" strip, with location blocked
- What I did, what I saw: With location blocked, the same message appears twice, once in red in the pill and again in the strip below, with a "Retry" link. Pressing Retry just fails again; nothing says how to allow location or that distances will be measured from City Hall instead.
- Which heuristic: 9, Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
- Screen or system: Screen. It knows the error code and could explain the fallback and how to allow location.
- Severity, and why: 2. Persistence drove it: the red message stays on every screen for the whole visit and Retry never changes it.
- The repair: One calm message says distances are from City Hall and how to turn on location if the visitor wants distances from where they are.

### Finding 8
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — Stores (store locator) window and card detail screen, with location blocked
- What I did, what I saw: The top bar says my location is unavailable, but the store locator marks Merlion Card Sanctum "Closest to You" and the card detail screen marks it "★ Nearest Location" and "950 m away". Both are measured from City Hall, not from me.
- Which heuristic: 4, Consistency and standards
- Screen or system: Screen. It already knows it is using the City Hall fallback.
- Severity, and why: 2. Impact drove it: a visitor far from City Hall picks a "closest" store that is not close to them.
- The repair: When the location is not known, labels read "closest to City Hall" (or similar), never "to you".

### Finding 9
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — card detail, cart and confirmation screens
- What I did, what I saw: The same action has different names on different screens: "Hold", "Add to Cart", "Check Out", then "Order Confirmed", "reservations have been dispatched" and "Total Paid / Due at Pickup". I could not tell whether I had bought, reserved or paid.
- Which heuristic: 4, Consistency and standards
- Screen or system: Screen. It is wording only.
- Severity, and why: 2. Impact drove it: confusion about whether money is owed at pickup is exactly what a buyer needs to be sure of.
- The repair: One word for the action is used from button to confirmation, and the confirmation says plainly whether anything has been paid.

### Finding 10
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — cart screen, "Clear Cart" button
- What I did, what I saw: "Clear Cart" sits next to "Back to Singles". One press empties the cart at once, with no confirmation and no undo.
- Which heuristic: 3, User control and freedom
- Screen or system: Screen.
- Severity, and why: 2. Impact drove it: it is rare, but a slip loses every item across every store.
- The repair: After clearing, the visitor can undo it, or is asked before the cart is emptied.

### Finding 11
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/cards/does-not-exist
- What I did, what I saw: I asked for an address that does not exist. Vercel's bare "404: NOT_FOUND" page appeared, with a code and no link back to the catalogue.
- Which heuristic: 9, Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
- Screen or system: System. The app never loads on that address, so no screen can help; it needs a hosting rule or a custom 404 page.
- Severity, and why: 2. Persistence drove it: every mistyped or old link lands on a dead end.
- The repair: An unknown address shows a page in the product's style with a link back to the catalogue.

### Finding 12
- Where: https://mgmt6110problemset3.vercel.app/ — card detail screen
- What I did, what I saw: The address stays the same on every screen. I could not bookmark or send a friend the page for one card; opening the address always starts at the catalogue.
- Which heuristic: 7, Flexibility and efficiency of use
- Screen or system: Screen (front-end routing), though the host must also send every card address to the app (see Finding 11).
- Severity, and why: 2. Frequency drove it: sharing "this card, this price" is a common thing for players to do.
- The repair: Each card has its own address that opens straight to its detail screen.

## 3. My predictions
1. The three findings I expect my groupmates to raise, and the severity I expect them to give each:
   - **Finding 3 (Cart button pushed off the phone screen): severity 3.** Anyone who opens the link on a phone and blocks location sees it within seconds, because the page slides sideways and there is no cart.
   - **Finding 1 (Back leaves the site and empties the cart): severity 3, possibly 4.** "Press back mid-task" is one of the set break tests, so every groupmate will try it, and the result is dramatic.
   - **Finding 4 (Hold says "Reserved" but nothing is held): severity 3.** The message makes a promise right next to the stock count, which visibly does not change.
2. The heuristic I think my product breaks worst: **1, Visibility of system status.** Five of my findings (3, 4, 6, 13, 14) come down to the product saying or showing something that does not match what actually happened, including messages that report reservations, additions and outages that did not happen.
3. The finding that would show my own evaluation was wrong: a groupmate who **allows** location on their phone reports that "Your Base" names an area they are not in, or that the store distances did not change from the City Hall figures. I ran every pass with location blocked, so I never saw the live-location path work; the area name comes from a short list of about fifteen reference points, so someone in, say, Jurong or Tampines could be told they are somewhere else. The same would be true if a groupmate rates Finding 1 at 1 or lower, because that would mean the Back problem does not matter the way I think it does.
